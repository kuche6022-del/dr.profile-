01 · WORK & WELLBEING

When being capable becomes exhausting

People who are dependable often become the people everyone turns to. Over time, being capable can quietly turn into carrying too much. You may keep delivering, solving and supporting while your own recovery time disappears.

A useful first step is to notice what is happening without judging yourself. Which demands are essential? Which expectations could be discussed? Where could a boundary, a pause or a more realistic workload make a difference?

Coaching can provide a structured space to examine those questions and identify practical changes. It is not a substitute for medical or mental-health care where that is needed.

02 · PURPOSE & CHANGE

Making room for a different chapter

A significant life or career transition can bring freedom and uncertainty at the same time. When familiar roles change, it can help to ask not only “What should I do next?” but also “What matters to me now?”

Try writing down three things you want more of, three things you are ready to leave behind and one small experiment you could try this month. The aim is not to map the rest of your life in one sitting; it is to create a next step you can learn from.

03 · YOUNG PEOPLE

Helping young people find their own direction

Young people do not always need another person to tell them what to do. Often, they need a respectful relationship in which they can be heard, recognise their strengths and explore options without being reduced to a label or a difficulty.

Helpful conversations can begin with curiosity: What has gone well recently? Who helps you feel supported? What would you like to be different? From there, a young person can begin to identify choices and practical next steps at a pace that feels manageable.
